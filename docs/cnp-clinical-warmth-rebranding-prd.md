# CNP Clinical Warmth Rebranding — Portfolio Case Study PRD

## Document Purpose

This PRD defines a 15-slide portfolio case study structure for **CNP Clinical Warmth Rebranding**. The deck should communicate the project as a strategic brand system, not a static packaging refresh. Every slide must prove one idea: clinical skincare becomes more usable when it is translated into a personal, skin-state-based experience.

## Narrative Thesis

**Clinical proof, made personal.**

CNP’s existing product experience is ingredient-led and product-name-led, while users make decisions through the condition they feel on their skin today. The rebrand reframes the brand around a warm but credible diagnostic system: four skin states, one coherent visual language, and a connected journey across packaging, digital, and popup experience.

## Target Reader

- Creative directors reviewing brand strategy depth
- Brand, BX, and visual design hiring teams
- Beauty and lifestyle stakeholders evaluating system thinking
- Portfolio reviewers who need to understand the designer’s role within 90 seconds

## Deck Principles

- Do not present the work as a decorative redesign.
- Lead with the user problem before showing visual assets.
- Show the system across touchpoints: packaging, UI, popup, and result card.
- Use concise English headlines with supporting Korean explanations where needed.
- Avoid generic portfolio labels such as “Overview,” “Contents,” or “Thank You.”

## Slide 02 — The Problem

### Headline

**Users don’t search by ingredients.**  
**They search by skin condition.**

### Problem Definition

The existing CNP experience is difficult to navigate because it is built around product groups, ingredients, and product names. Users who are not dermatology experts struggle to immediately understand which product matches what their skin needs today.

### Key Points

- Too many product lines compete for attention.
- Ingredient-centered navigation requires prior skincare knowledge.
- Product-name-centered structures do not answer the user’s first question: “What does my skin need right now?”

### Visual & Layout Direction

- Use a before/after diagram.
- Left side: fragmented product and ingredient taxonomy.
- Right side: simplified skin-state decision structure.
- Keep the page mostly monochrome with one warm accent color to signal the coming transition.

### Visual & Layout Direction

## Slide 06 — Brand Architecture

### Headline

**4 Skin-State System**

### Lines

| Skin-State Line | State | System Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning After | Tired, dull, and unbalanced skin | Recovery and glow |
| Stress Signal | Sensitive, reactive, stressed skin | Calming and protection |
| City Dryness | Dryness caused by urban lifestyle | Hydration and barrier support |
| Reset Night | Skin needing overnight recovery | Renewal and ritual |

### Core Message

The system reorganizes exploration by skin state, not by product category.

### Visual & Layout Direction

- Present four vertical cards, one per skin-state line.
- Each card should include state, color, signature graphic, representative product, and key message.
- Avoid making the slide look like a SKU list; it should feel like a diagnostic map.
